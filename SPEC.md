# SPEC: timeline-testbed (v0.2, 2026-10-21)

**Status:** v0.2 adopts the M0 spec review (`reports/M0-spec-review.md`) and the owner's decisions on
§12 (DECISIONS.md, 2026-10-21).

- Items marked **[default]** are proposals. The implementer may challenge them, and the owner
  settles them in DECISIONS.md.
- §2 (question and claim) and §3 (non-goals) change only with the owner's approval.

## 1. Why this project exists

The predecessor, EntitlementShield (`~/code/entitlement-shield`), built an abuse detector for a
fictional free-credit product. For eight weeks it tried to show that an LLM second stage adds value,
and every result was exploratory or null. The main reason was that none of its evaluation worlds
could answer the question:

- **Its own generator:** circular. The detector scored P = R = 1.0.
- **Worlds other LLMs wrote from a brief:** either barely learnable (Tier 4, supervised ceiling AUC
  0.67) or broken (a perfect shortcut; one uniform label shift).
- **The allowed-unit pool:** only 4 independent abusive groups.

`context/lessons.md` has the summary and `context/prior-results.md` the numbers.

This project answers the question on a testbed built for it: a world generator we own, where each
abuse mechanism, where its evidence lives and how strong it is are all declared in advance. It's a
known-answer test of the method, and it says nothing about real-world abuse.

## 2. The question and the claim

**Setting (inherited).** StudioAI gives each person 25 free credits per 30-day cycle. Abuse means one
person running several accounts to collect extra allocations. Households, classrooms and offices
legitimately share devices, cards and networks.

**The questions:**
- **Q1.** When the only evidence that separates one operator from several real people is in the
  activity timeline, can a small local LLM find it where the rules detector and feature-based models
  can't?
- **Q2.** Does fine-tuning on labelled outcomes beat zero-shot use, and a linear probe on the same
  model?
- **Q3.** Does what it learned carry over to abuse mechanisms it never saw in training?

**The claim has this form, and nothing stronger:**

> On a synthetic testbed with declared mechanisms, at signal strength *s*, on these *k* held-out abuse
> families whose evidence is timeline-only, <method> reaches AUC *a* [95% group-bootstrap CI], against
> *b* for the stronger of two models over the predecessor's 64 features (logistic, boosted trees) and
> *c* for the frozen detector.

Results are curves over *s*, broken out per channel (§5.4) and per regime (§6.3). A null is a valid,
publishable outcome: "even when the signal is only in the timeline, the LLM adds nothing over features
at this scale."

The claim covers only the catalogue's families. The bootstrap is over groups, conditional on those
families, so it says nothing about other mechanisms. Every result is reported per family as well as
pooled.

## 3. Non-goals

- **Realism or external validity.** No production-style metrics.
- **Hosted or paid models, or GPU rentals.** Model downloads and package installs happen only with the
  owner's go.
- **Changing or retuning the vendored detector.** It's a frozen baseline.
- **Appeals, reversal automation, the API and console, and demo work.**
- **Linkage research.** The testbed keeps linkage easy (§5.5), so it tests the scoring stage.
  Fragmentation is a later question.
- **An ambiguous class.** Every label is abusive or legitimate.

## 5. The testbed generator

### 5.1 Principles (all required)

1. **One operator versus several people is the mechanism.** Abuse is generated by one controller
   acting through several accounts. Legitimate groups are several independent people sharing
   resources.
2. **Every abusive family has its own mechanism and a legitimate twin.** There's no label shift shared
   across families (that was codex-02's failure). Each family implements its own description, and its
   twin is the legitimate scenario it's most easily mistaken for.
3. **Matched nuisance.** Within a twin pair, every variable other than the mechanism is drawn from the
   same distribution:
   - group size;
   - account creation dates and age;
   - email domain type and verification level;
   - linkage signal type and reliability;
   - total activity volume and number of cycles;
   - context-only signals (`network_group`, `behavioral_template`): presence, type and sharing;
   - event-type mix, event count (and so render length), login count, endpoint cost tier, and
     verification events with their timing relative to claims;
   - every other persisted field the mechanism doesn't name.

   Abusive prevalence is the same in every pair, so the pair itself carries no label information.
4. **A declared evidence channel.** Each family is tagged F, T or M:
   - **F:** visible to the detector's 12 features;
   - **T:** timeline-only, invisible to the 12 features by construction;
   - **M:** mixed.

   Tags are verified (§5.4, §5.6) and frozen at M1.
5. **One strength knob per family, *s* ∈ [0, 1].**
   - At *s* = 0, the abusive family is generated by exactly its twin's process, so nothing can tell
     them apart. That's the negative control.
   - At *s* = 1, the mechanism is applied in full, with the family's stated jitter.
   - In between, *s* scales a per-unit probability (per session, day or hand-off) or a coupling
     strength whose zero is independence. It never scales jitter around a shared template, because
     "full jitter" is not the twin's process.
6. **Deterministic.** The output bytes depend only on the generator version, seed, *s* and scale. Use
   the vendored `canonical_bytes` for the checksum.
7. **Opaque, label-free inference data.** Ids, row order and timestamps carry no label or family
   information. Vendored ids are `acct-<sha256>`; follow that pattern.
8. **Emit the vendored data model.** Build a vendored `GeneratedWorld` and persist it with the vendored
   `persist_world` and the write-only ground-truth sink (wiring as in `scripts/seed.py`).
   - Labels: `GenLabel(cluster_id=<true group>, scenario_family, label)`.
   - Split groups: `GenSplit(account_id, split_group_id=<true group>)`.
9. **T mechanisms are couplings of the twin.** A T family draws its twin's process, then changes
   only cross-account order or dependence. It must leave the joint distribution of everything the 12
   detector features read unchanged:
   - each account's consumption days, and the days shared across accounts, in the trailing 60 days;
   - each account's hourly and daily consumption against the banding rate limits, and its 168-bin
     hour-by-weekday histogram;
   - claim, exhaustion and signup times; verification events and their order relative to claims;
     login counts;
   - email domain, verification level, age and linkage.

   Two constraints make this easy to meet:
   - T pairs stay under the banding rate limits (≤ 8 credits per clock hour, ≤ 20 per day), so the
     rate axis never fails.
   - A T mechanism changes only consume events: their amounts, or their times within a session that
     fits inside one clock hour.
   - No T-pair account signs up inside F-CYC-3's 60-day recency window, so moving an exhaustion time
     can't fire it.

   The §5.4 check verifies the result.

### 5.2 Family catalogue [default]

| Pair | Abusive family (one operator) | What *s* controls | Legitimate twin (several people) | Channel | Oracle statistic (label-free) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sequential rotator.** Accounts are used one at a time; the next account's first claim follows the previous account's exhaustion within hours | Probability that a hand-off follows the rule (otherwise the next start is independent) | **Shared-device household.** Members take turns on one device, with independent start times | F | Median gap from one account's exhaustion to the next account's first claim |
| 2 | **Scripted parallel farm.** One operator steps through all accounts in each session, always in the same account order | Probability per session that the accounts' start order follows the fixed order (otherwise the twin's random order) | **Classroom lab.** Concurrent scheduled sessions; each student's start offset and in-session sequence are their own | T | Rank concordance of account start order within sessions, across sessions |
| 3 | **Template operator.** Every account replays the operator's session script: the same consume amounts and gaps | Probability per session that an account replays the operator's template session (otherwise it draws its own, as the twin does) | **Small office team on a shared card.** A similar schedule; each person draws their own session amounts and gaps from the same distribution | T | Similarity of within-session amount and gap sequences across members |
| 4 | **Single-budget "family".** Each account has a staggered schedule (so banding counts several streams), but one budget: when one account uses more on a day, the others use less | Strength of a negative rank coupling of daily amounts across accounts, on each account's own active days (0 = independent) | **Real heavy-use family.** Independent members with uncorrelated daily consumption | T | Variance of the combined daily consumption relative to the sum of member variances |
| 5 | **Established-account expansion.** One aged account with normal logins, plus new accounts that copy its daily rhythm and spending and start after it reaches its cap | How closely the copy and the timing follow the rule | **Established user adds a relative.** The newcomer has an independent rhythm and start time | M | Rhythm distance from each newcomer to the main account, plus start-after-cap timing |
| 6 | **Serial session operator.** In shared sessions, one operator works the accounts one at a time, so each account's consume events form one unbroken block | Probability per shared session that the events are rearranged into per-account blocks (otherwise the twin's interleaving) | **Roommates sharing a payment card.** They use the service in the same sessions; each consumes independently, so their events interleave | T | Account switches among consume events per shared session, relative to the minimum |

Pair 6 replaced v0.1's low-and-slow operator: "never active on the same day" is exactly what F-CYC-2
tests, so that family was F.

**The couplings for the T pairs (§5.1(9)).** Each session fits inside one clock hour.
- **Pair 2:** draw the twin's session (each account's start offset and in-session sequence), then
  reassign the start offsets so the starts follow the fixed order.
- **Pair 3:** the template session is one draw from the twin's per-session distribution. Session times
  and the number of sessions per account come from the twin.
- **Pair 4:** active days and times come from the twin. A rank coupling (copula) changes only how daily
  amounts co-vary across accounts, keeping each account's marginal.
- **Pair 6:** keep each account's events and amounts, and reassign the session's consume times so each
  account's events are contiguous.

**Rules for the catalogue:**
- **One sentence per family.** For each family, write one sentence that says exactly what *s* scales
  (a probability per session, or a coupling strength), and implement exactly that. For a T family,
  the sentence also says what is drawn from the twin and what is changed.
- **The twin doesn't depend on *s*.** A twin's behaviour never changes with *s*.
- **The oracle statistic is the bound.** It measures the family's mechanism directly from inference
  data, without labels. It's the upper bound, not a competitor, and it's never a feature for any rung.

### 5.3 Fixed twin parameters

Twins can have partial coherence of their own: a classroom shares a schedule, and a household shares
a weekly rhythm. That coherence is fixed per twin and documented, and it doesn't vary with *s*. It's
what makes the pair hard at intermediate *s*.

### 5.4 Channel check

The tag is measured, then frozen at M1.

- **Scores.** For each family against its twin, at *s* = 1:
  - the AUC of a logistic model over the 12 F-* raw values, under 5-fold group CV;
  - the same for boosted trees (`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, defaults);
  - the AUC of the detector score, with no fitting.

  One-sided bounds come from the group bootstrap (§6.4).
- **Sample.** The T-designed pairs run on a dedicated channel-check world at *s* = 1, with 200
  abusive and 800 legitimate groups per pair. At the standard 40 + 160, the AUC's standard error is
  0.05, too wide to tell 0.5 from 0.6. The F and M tags use the two standard *s* = 1 worlds, pooled.
- **T:** all three scores have a one-sided 95% upper bound ≤ 0.60. A family designed as T that fails
  gets its mechanism changed, or is re-tagged, before the M1 freeze.
- **F:** the better F12 model reaches ≥ 0.80.
- **M:** anything else.
- **Also recorded:** per-feature AUCs, for diagnosis, and each family's F64-w90 AUC at *s* = 1,
  reported but not part of the tag.

### 5.5 Linkage

- Every group shares at least one edge-forming resource signal (device, payment token or recovery
  contact; see the vendored `SignalType`), at a reliability the vendored clustering accepts.
- No signal value is shared across groups.
- Single-account groups: none [default].
- The check: at least 95% of groups come out of the detector as exactly one cluster, and no cluster
  mixes two groups.

### 5.6 World health checks

These must pass on every world used for a reported number. They read labels, so they live in
`testbed/eval/health.py`. Each run writes `runs/<world>/health.json`.

| Check | Pass |
|---|---|
| Mechanical validity: no event or observation before account creation; consumption ≤ grant per cycle; cycles inside the window; unique ids | All |
| Determinism: regenerate and compare canonical bytes | Identical |
| Order and id coupling: AUC of the label against id sort order, row order and creation order | Null rule |
| Nuisance balance per twin pair: AUC of each §5.1(3) variable, family against twin, including event count | Null rule, on a generator-only sample and on each world |
| Negative control, *s* = 0: oracle statistic AUC per family against its twin | Null rule, on a generator-only sample and on each *s* = 0 world |
| Positive control, *s* = 1: oracle statistic AUC per family against its twin | ≥ 0.90 |
| Channel check (§5.4) | As §5.4 |
| Linkage recovery (§5.5) | ≥ 95% one-to-one; 0 mixed clusters |
| Render visibility: the oracle statistic recomputed from the render text alone, per family | AUC ≥ 0.85 at *s* = 1; at *s* = 0.75, within 0.05 of the full-data oracle |
| Render length: AUC of the token count, family against twin, per pair | Null rule |
| Information parity (§6.2) | Every F64-w90 value is recomputed from the render text by a test |

- **The null rule:** the Bonferroni-adjusted 95% CI contains 0.5. The adjustment counts every test in
  that row on that world or sample.
  - At 40 + 160 groups the AUC's standard error at chance is 0.05, so a fixed 0.45–0.55 band would
    fail about a third of fair comparisons.
  - Each rule fails a fair generator about 5% of the time at most.
- **Generator-only sample:** at least 2,000 groups per side, generated without persisting or
  detecting. The sampler is checked at a scale where the CI is about ±0.03. The per-world checks
  then catch persistence and wiring faults.

### 5.7 Scale and sweep (owner, 2026-10-21)

- 6 twin pairs × (40 abusive + 160 legitimate) = 1,200 groups per world, 20% abusive.
- Plus one channel-check world per generator version (§5.4): the four T pairs, each with 200 + 800
  groups, at *s* = 1.
- 2–6 accounts per group.
- 3 cycles (90 days), ending at the vendored `WORLD_END`.
- Activity sized so that a group's render fits in 2,048 tokens (§6.2).
- Sweep *s* ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, with two development seeds per *s* for the cheap rungs.
- M0 measures detector runtime. Keep one world's full pipeline (generate, persist, detect, units)
  under about 30 minutes. If it runs over, shrink the activity before shrinking the group counts.

## 6. Units, inputs and metrics

### 6.1 Unit

- There's one unit per detector cluster, at `as_of = WORLD_END`. Its record holds the member ids,
  every cycle payload, the detector score and the latest payload.
- The unit's label is its group's label.
- Clusters that mix groups are excluded and counted. With §5.5 there should be none.
- The detector score for a unit is the maximum `risk_score` over the cluster's cycle units in that
  run.
- Cycles anchor at each account's creation, as in the vendored generator, so a cluster has several
  cycle units, one per distinct cycle start.
  - The latest payload is the one with the latest `window_start`.
  - It may be a partial cycle; that's matched within pairs.

### 6.2 Inputs per rung, and information parity

No rung sees data after `WORLD_END` or anything derived from labels. The F64 features and the render
cover the same window, [`WORLD_END` − 90 d, `WORLD_END`). Both are read from the inference store, not
from the payload timeline.

- **Detector:** its own payloads.
- **F12:** the 12 F-* raw values from the latest payload (§6.1) [default].
- **F64-w90:** the predecessor's 64 features (`scripts/p6_4_features.py`, `unit_features`), adapted
  so the window is the 90 days above instead of one cycle.
  - Same names and count as the predecessor's.
  - Every feature has a known-answer test.
- **LLM rungs:** one fixed render per unit, ≤ 2,048 tokens including the prompt [default].
  - Tokens are counted with the tokenizer of each model that reads the render.
  - **A new raw-event render:**
    - one line per event (relative day, HH:MM, account alias, event type, amount);
    - the account and shared-resource headers;
    - no derived cross-account statistics.
  - **The vendored v2 and v3 renders can't be used.** They keep one row per (day, account, event
    type), with only the first time and the day's total. That erases what pairs 2, 3 and 6 depend
    on.
  - Activity is sized so the largest group fits.
  - The render is frozen before M4 and shared by R1–R3.
  - It must pass the render-visibility and render-length checks (§5.6).
- **Information parity:** every F64-w90 input can be recomputed from the render text. A test checks
  it, so the LLM rungs see at least what F64 sees.

### 6.3 Regimes

- **In-family.** 5-fold group cross-validation over all families, stratified by label and family;
  each group sits in exactly one fold [default]. This measures how learnable the seen mechanisms are.
- **Held-out family.** This is the primary regime for Q3. There are two folds (owner, 2026-10-21):
  - **Fold A** trains on pairs 1, 2 and 3 and tests on pairs 4, 5 and 6.
  - **Fold B** is the reverse.
  - Each side holds two T families: {2, 3} and {4, 6}.
  - Zero-shot rungs are scored on the same test sets, so the numbers compare.
- **Pooling worlds.** When two worlds share an *s*, their groups are pooled: each fold trains on both
  training sides and tests on both test sides.

### 6.4 Metrics

- **Primary:** AUC on T families in the held-out regime at *s* = 0.75 (owner, 2026-10-21). *s* = 0.5
  is secondary.
  - Per fold: the AUC of the test side's T abusive groups against their own twins, scored by that
    fold's model.
  - The primary number is the mean of the two fold AUCs.
- **Also reported:**
  - AUC per family and per channel;
  - recall at a false-positive rate of 5% and 10%;
  - every regime at every *s*;
  - each rung against the family's measured oracle AUC, because *s* means different things in
    different families.
- **Uncertainty:**
  - 95% intervals from a bootstrap over groups, resampled within each fold (2,000 resamples
    [default]);
  - differences between rungs from a paired bootstrap on the same resamples.
- **Context:** every report shows random (0.5) and the oracle bound next to each number.

## 7. The model ladder

| Rung | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| R0 | Baselines: random; detector score; F12 logistic; F64-w90 logistic; F64-w90 boosted trees (`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, defaults); oracle bound | Copy `_models()` and the CV pattern from `scripts/p6_4_ceiling.py`, with folds as in §6.3 |
| R1 | Zero-shot: local Qwen 3.5 9B with one fixed prompt that returns `RISK_LEVEL: 1-10`; Nimble (typed score) | Runners exist: `scripts/p6_recall_pilot.py` (`run`, against the local OpenAI-compatible MTPLX server) and `scripts/p6_3_nimble_run.py` (MLX venv). Reported, but not gating. To save compute, score *s* ∈ {0.5, 0.75} with one seed [default] |
| R2 | Linear probe: pooled hidden states of a local model over the render (forward passes only), into an L2 logistic model on the same folds | Runs on the cached Qwen 3.5 9B and on the R3 model (owner, 2026-10-21). [default] Candidates: the mean-pooled final-layer state, one middle layer, and the final-token state. One R2 run at *s* = 0 is the LLM rungs' negative control |
| R3 | LoRA fine-tune (MLX) that emits the label. Score = P(abusive), from the answer tokens | Only if G2 passes. Train once per held-out fold. A Qwen 3.5 of about 4B (owner, 2026-10-21); its download is decided at M3, on measured numbers. One training run takes ≤ 12 h [default] |

**Why R2 comes before R3.** A linear probe asks, for a few hours of forward passes, whether the
model's representation of the render holds the signal at all. If it doesn't beat F64, fine-tuning
probably won't either, and the line stops.

### 7.1 Gates and stop rules

- **G1, end of M2: is the testbed valid?**
  - Every §5.6 check passes on the sweep worlds.
  - R0 behaves as designed:
    - the oracle reaches ≥ 0.90 at *s* = 1;
    - every R0 rung meets the null rule (§5.6) at *s* = 0, over the two *s* = 0 worlds pooled;
    - the T tags from §5.4 hold.
  - G1 can't test the LLM rungs; M4's *s* = 0 run of R2 does.
  - If G1 fails, fix the generator, at most twice. Then stop and report to the owner.
- **G2, end of M4: the kill gate.** It's pre-registered in `prereg/M4.md` before scoring.
  - It passes if, on the primary metric (§6.4) at *s* = 0.75, R2 beats both F64-w90 models (logistic
    and boosted trees), each with a paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound above 0.
  - Both *s* = 0.75 development seeds are used, pooled (§6.3).
  - A pass counts only if R2's *s* = 0 run shows no signal (its 95% CI contains 0.5). Otherwise, find
    the leak first.
  - If it fails, stop the LLM line and write the result up (M7). R1 never rescues a failed G2.
- **G3, end of M5.** It's pre-registered in `prereg/M5.md`.
  - It passes if R3 beats R2 (run on R3's own model) and both F64-w90 models on the same comparison.
  - It's reported either way, and it decides what goes to M6.
- **No moving goalposts.** A gate's threshold can't change after its result is seen. A
  pre-registration can be amended only before its scoring run, with a dated line saying why.

## 8. Confirmation (M6)

1. **Freeze everything** and record the hashes in `prereg/M6.md`, along with the comparisons and
   thresholds:
   - the generator version and its code hash;
   - the render;
   - every model artifact: weights, adapters, probe coefficients and prompts;
   - the scorer.

   `prereg/M6.md` gets one cross-vendor review round.
2. **Draw fresh seeds after the freeze**, with Python's `secrets` module: one world at *s* = 0.5 and
   one at *s* = 0.75. Record the seeds in DECISIONS.md. The ground-truth stores of the confirmation
   worlds are opened only by the M6 scorer, once, after the freeze.
3. **Score once.** Report everything that was pre-registered, whatever it shows.
4. **Optional, with the owner's go at the time; default no (owner, 2026-10-21), revisited only if G3
   passed:** a one-time transfer check on the predecessor's sealed `tier4-v2` test split, using the
   frozen winner. It runs from the old repo through its
   finalization path. Tier 4's families are different and its ceiling is 0.67, so expect little; the
   value is an honest out-of-distribution readout.

**A lightweight seal.** `testbed/eval/labels.py` refuses to open a ground-truth store under
`runs/confirm-*` unless `prereg/M6.md` exists and its recorded hashes match the current artifacts.

## 9. Leakage controls

- **Two stores, as in the predecessor.** `DATABASE_URL` and `GROUND_TRUTH_DATABASE_URL` are separate,
  and both are always passed explicitly.
- **Only `testbed/eval/` opens a ground-truth store.**
  - `testbed/world/` may use the vendored write-only sink.
  - Labels reach model code only as arguments passed in from `testbed/eval/`.
  - A test scans the imports and string literals in `testbed/` to enforce this. The pattern is the
    vendored `backend/app/tests/test_ground_truth_boundary.py`.
- **No rung's input contains family names, group ids, *s* or seeds.** Add a test that renders contain
  no family names.
- **The implementer knows every mechanism.** That's accepted, because this is a known-answer test.
  The guards against fooling yourself are:
  - the negative control;
  - the held-out regime;
  - the frozen render and the frozen gates;
  - the M6 confirmation worlds.

## 11. Compute notes

These were measured in the predecessor on 2026-10-16. Re-measure them in M3.

- **Local LoRA (MLX, Qwen 3.5 9B, 4-bit):**
  - works at 1k tokens (37 tok/s) and at 2k tokens (≈ 20 tok/s, 34 GiB);
  - fails at 4k tokens and above (`metal::malloc Resource limit`).
- **Scoring:** an 18k-token prompt in 56 s, so roughly 300 prompt tok/s.
- **Probe estimate (9B):** 1,200 units × 2k tokens ≈ 2.4M tokens, about 2 h per world.
- **R3 estimate (9B, 4-bit):** per fold, about 600 groups × 1.5k tokens ≈ 0.9M tokens per epoch,
  about 7–12 h at 20–37 tok/s. A smaller model would be several times cheaper; M3 decides, and any
  download needs the owner's go.
- **Tokenization:** "10" tokenizes as "1" and "0", so scoring a 1–10 answer needs terminated
  candidates. Labels as single tokens (for example "A" or "B") avoid the problem.
- **An open issue from the predecessor:** a cached scorer differed from a full forward pass by 0.12
  nats on one candidate. Check scorer agreement in M3.
- **Nimble:** hard input cap of 8,192 tokens; trained on prompts of 2,048 tokens or fewer; peak memory
  20.9 GiB.
